Frequently Asked Questions

General#

Can't Zendesk do this natively? Not in the same way. Zendesk can send automated emails, but it can't put a functional, one-click action button inside them that updates the ticket when the customer clicks. That is exactly what Klick-Zen adds.

Do I need a developer to use Klick-Zen? No. Buttons are created in the Klick-Zen app and added to Zendesk by pasting a snippet into a trigger, automation, or macro. No coding is required. The one place you work with template code is the Help Center ticket view, and even there you are only pasting a provided snippet.

What ticket actions can a button perform? A button can update the ticket status, add tags, add a public or private comment, assign the ticket to an agent or group, change the priority, and change the ticket type. See Button Actions.

Can one button perform multiple actions? Yes. A single button can carry several actions, and all of them are applied to the ticket in one pass when the customer clicks. For example, one click can set the ticket to Solved, add a tag, and post a comment.

Is any additional hosting required? No. Klick-Zen hosts your button images and the post-click landing page for you, so there is nothing extra to set up or maintain.


Appearance and customization#

Can I customize how buttons and the landing page look? Yes. You control each button's text, font, colors, border, and roundness, and you can brand the hosted landing page with your colors, logo, and message. See Button Appearance and Landing Pages.

Can I use my own image or a plain text link instead of the Klick-Zen button? Yes. The snippet is just a link wrapped around an image, so you can swap in your own hosted image, or remove the image and use a text link. The action still fires on click. See How do I use my own button image?.

Can I serve buttons from my own domain? Yes, using the Host Mapping setting. Once you add the required DNS records and validate the domain, buttons and links are served from your subdomain instead of the default Klick-Zen host. See How do I use my own domain name?.


Actions, triggers, and workflows#

Does this work with my existing Zendesk triggers and automations? Yes, and this is where a lot of the power is. A button click updates the ticket through the Zendesk API, so it is treated exactly like any other ticket change. Your existing triggers and automations evaluate it normally, which means a button can add a tag and a downstream trigger can route, notify, or reply based on that tag. See How do I enhance button actions by firing triggers?.

Can a button change the assignee or priority directly? Yes. Assigning the ticket and changing priority are both built-in button actions, so a button can reassign or re-prioritize a ticket on click without any additional setup.

What happens if the customer never clicks a button? Nothing changes. A button is optional for the customer. The ticket simply stays as it is, and your normal workflow continues, so there is no downside to including one.

Can I make a button work only once? Yes. Add an action that applies a tag (for example button_clicked) and an exception that checks for that same tag. The first click applies the tag and runs the actions; every later click matches the exception and does nothing. See How do I ensure a button is only clicked once?.

Can I stop a button from working? Yes. Uncheck Button Enabled in the button's settings. A disabled button renders in greyscale, takes no action when clicked, and simply sends the customer to the landing page. See How do I prevent a button from being used?.


Permissions and limits#

Who can create or change buttons? Only Zendesk admins can create, edit, clone, delete or import buttons, change Settings, or open billing. Agents can still insert existing buttons from the ticket editor.

Is there a limit to how many buttons I can have? Yes. An account can have up to 100 buttons. Deleted buttons don't count toward the limit, so removing buttons you no longer use frees up room. Each button can also have up to 6 actions and 6 exceptions.

Can I organize my buttons once I have a lot of them? Yes. Give buttons labels and use the search, label filter, action/exception filter and sort tools on the button list. A label can also drive which buttons agents see in the ticket editor.


Security and access#

Is it secure to let customers update tickets without logging in? Yes. Each button link is unique to a single ticket and is delivered only in that ticket's email thread, the same trust model as replying to a Zendesk email, which already updates the ticket. A click can only perform the specific actions you configured; it cannot read ticket contents, reach other tickets, or do anything you did not set up.

Does the customer need a Help Center account or login to click a button? No. The button works straight from the email with no login and no Help Center account. See How do I let customers update a ticket without logging in?.

What if the customer forwards the email to someone else? Then that person can click the button, the same way they could forward the email and reply to update the ticket that way. The click carries the same trust as the email thread it arrived in. Where that matters, such as approvals or anything financial, pair the button with a check your process already trusts rather than relying on the click alone.

Is this the same as two-factor authentication? No. Klick-Zen is not an identity-verification or 2FA product. It gives the recipient of a ticket's email a trusted, one-click way to act on that ticket. For actions that require stronger assurance, combine the button with your own verification step.

What gets recorded on the ticket when a button is clicked? Whatever actions you configured: any tags, comments, or field changes the button applies appear on the ticket like any other update. If you enable Log Issue as Private Comment, a failed click also leaves a private comment explaining what went wrong.

Can an email security scanner or link prefetching trigger a button? No. Many email systems open links automatically, either security scanners checking for threats or email clients prefetching links before anyone clicks. Klick-Zen recognizes these automated visits and ignores them. A button's actions run only when a person actually clicks it, so a ticket won't be closed, rated, or rerouted just because an email was delivered or scanned.

Do I need to allowlist Klick-Zen in our email security tools? Usually not. Scanners and prefetchers can open button links as usual; they just don't run the button's actions. If your security team prefers links that point to a domain you already trust, set up a custom domain (see below), and button links and images will come from your own company domain instead of Klick-Zen's.

Can button links and images use our own domain? Yes. Point a subdomain of your company domain (for example, buttons.yourcompany.com) at Klick-Zen with a CNAME record, and your button links, button images and hosted landing page are served from it. Customers see a link from a domain they recognize, and your email security team only has to trust a domain you own. See Custom domain setup for the steps.


Reporting#

Can I report on button activity in Zendesk Explore? Yes. Because buttons apply tags and field changes to the ticket, that data flows into Zendesk Explore alongside the rest of your ticket data. Tagging each outcome (for example csat_good, refund_approved, route_billing) gives you clean, reportable results.


Common use cases#

Can I build a CSAT survey with buttons? Yes. Place rating buttons directly in the solved-ticket email so the customer rates in one click, with no separate survey page. Each button records the score as a tag and, if you like, a custom field. See How do I create a one-click CSAT?.

Can I use my own rating scale or button styles for CSAT? Yes. Because you design the buttons, you can use a 2-point, 5-point, or any other scale, styled however you want. This is a common reason teams choose Klick-Zen CSAT over the native satisfaction rating.

Can a bad rating automatically trigger a follow-up? Yes. Have the low-rating button add a tag such as csat_bad, then use a Zendesk trigger on that tag to reopen the ticket, raise priority, and notify a lead. The rating stops being just a number and becomes a recovery workflow.

What if the customer picks the wrong category when self-routing? The same as today, only sooner and cheaper to fix. An agent reassigns the ticket as they would any misrouted request, and the tag shows it happened, so if one category is chosen incorrectly often, you will see it in Explore and can reword the button. In practice, customers categorize their own requests more accurately than keyword rules do.

Won't customers click "escalate" on everything? In practice, no, and even if a few do, you are no worse off than a customer replying "this is urgent" today. The difference is that the click is structured: it tags and routes the ticket so your team can see and manage escalations, rather than scanning replies for urgency. You can also add exceptions so an escalate button does nothing on tickets that are already escalated or closed.


Internal and approval workflows#

Can I use buttons internally, for staff approvals? Yes. Place a button in an internal note and it becomes a one-click control for your team, for approvals, escalations, sign-offs, or handoffs, that the customer never sees. See How do I use buttons for internal routing and approvals?.

Can I require manager approval before something proceeds? Yes, and the workflow enforces it rather than relying on memory. Route requests to a holding view, have the approval button add a tag such as approved, and use a trigger that releases the ticket to the next step only when that tag appears. The approval is recorded on the ticket as a timestamped private note.

Is a button click enough for an approval audit trail? The click itself is one signal; pair it with a private comment action so each decision is written to the ticket, optionally under a specific comment delegate account. Combined with the tag each button adds, you get a reportable, timestamped record of who approved or denied what.


Billing-related buttons#

Can a button actually cancel or change a subscription in my billing system? Not on its own. A button acts on the Zendesk ticket. It can capture the customer's intent (for example tag the ticket renewal_confirmed or cancel_requested) and route it, but it does not reach into your billing system unless you connect that step yourself through your own trigger or integration.

Does Klick-Zen connect to our billing or other external systems? Klick-Zen performs actions within Zendesk. To extend a click into another system, use the tag or ticket change the button makes as the hook for your existing Zendesk integrations or automations.
