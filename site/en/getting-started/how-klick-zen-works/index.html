How Klick-Zen Works

Klick-Zen buttons look simple to your customers: a styled button in an email that they click once. Behind that click is a coordinated system of three components; an HTML snippet embedded in your Zendesk emails, a set of Klick-Zen hosted assets (button images and the landing page), and the Klick-Zen action service, which securely applies your configured actions to the ticket via the Zendesk API.

This below explains how those pieces fit together, what actually gets inserted into an email, and exactly what happens the moment a customer clicks.


The HTML Snippet#

When you create a button, Klick-Zen generates a small block of HTML. At its core, the snippet is simply a hyperlink wrapped around a hosted button image:

  • An anchor (<a>) element whose href points to a unique Klick-Zen action URL for that button
  • An image (<img>) element whose src points to the button graphic, rendered and hosted by Klick-Zen

Because the button is delivered as a real image rather than styled HTML/CSS, it displays consistently across email clients. Gmail, Outlook, Apple Mail, and mobile apps all show the same button, with no broken styling or fallback rendering.

You paste this snippet into any Zendesk email surface: a trigger or automation email template, a macro, or an individual ticket reply (via the Klick-Zen toolbar button in the ticket editor).


Zendesk Placeholders: Connecting the Button to the Ticket#

A button is only useful if Klick-Zen knows which ticket to act on. This is where Zendesk placeholders come in.

The action URL inside the snippet includes Zendesk placeholder syntax, the same double-curly-brace variables used throughout Zendesk email templates, such as {{ticket.id}}. In the template, the snippet's link looks similar to this:

<a href="https://buttons.klick-zen.com/ext/href/f2a782f8/{{ticket.id}}/{{ticket.encoded.id}}">
  <img src="https://buttons.klick-zen.com/ext/src/f2a782f8/{{ticket.id}}" />
</a>

The bottom line: the snippet you paste into a template is generic, but every email sent from it is ticket-specific. At send time, Zendesk's notification engine substitutes each placeholder with the real value for that ticket. The email that lands in the customer's inbox therefore contains a fully resolved link, one that references a specific ticket (for example, ticket 45821) and uniquely ties that button, in that email, to that customer's ticket.

This is why the same snippet can live in a trigger that fires thousands of times; Zendesk personalizes the link on every send, with no per-ticket work on your part.


The Klick-Zen Action Service#

The action URL doesn't point at Zendesk - it points at Klick-Zen. When your customer clicks, their browser makes a request to Klick-Zen's servers, which hold everything needed to complete the update:

  • The button definition, which is the list of actions you configured (update status, add tags, insert a comment, assign, change priority or type)
  • The authorized connection to your Zendesk instance, established when you installed and authorized the app

Klick-Zen uses that authorized connection to apply your actions through the Zendesk API on the ticket identified in the link. The customer never has to log in to your help center, and no credentials are ever exposed in the email.


What Gets Inserted Into an Automated Email#

Putting it together, here's the lifecycle of a button inside a Zendesk trigger email:

  1. You paste the snippet once. The Klick-Zen HTML (the anchor link with its placeholder-bearing action URL, plus the hosted image) goes into the email body of a trigger, automation, or macro.
  2. A ticket event fires the trigger. For example, an agent solves a ticket and the "Notify requester" trigger runs.
  3. Zendesk renders the template. All placeholders in the email, including the ones inside the Klick-Zen URL, are replaced with that ticket's live values. The generic snippet becomes a ticket-specific link.
  4. The email is delivered. The customer's email client fetches the button image from Klick-Zen's servers and displays it inline. To the customer, it's just a clean, branded button embedded within the message.

Nothing about the ticket is exposed in the button itself beyond what's encoded in the link, and the image is identical for every recipient. Only the link is personalized.


What Happens When the Button Is Clicked#

The click sets off a fast, fully automated sequence:

  1. The browser opens the action URL. Clicking the button issues a standard HTTPS request to Klick-Zen, carrying the button identifier and the ticket reference that Zendesk substituted at send time.
  2. Klick-Zen validates the request. The service confirms the link corresponds to a real button in your account and a valid ticket reference before doing anything else.
  3. The configured actions execute. Klick-Zen calls the Zendesk API over your authorized connection and applies every action assigned to the button in a single pass. One click can, for example, set the ticket to Solved, apply a customer_confirmed tag, and add a private comment noting that the customer closed the ticket via email.
  4. Zendesk business rules react. Because the update arrives through the Zendesk API, it is indistinguishable from any other ticket update. That means your existing triggers and automations evaluate it normally. A tag added by a button click can route the ticket, notify a supervisor, or send a follow-up email, exactly as if an agent had made the change. This trigger cascade is what makes Klick-Zen composable with the rest of your Zendesk workflow.

  5. The customer is redirected. With the actions applied, Klick-Zen immediately redirects the browser to one of two destinations:

    • Your Klick-Zen hosted landing page: a confirmation page customized with your colors, logo, and message, served by Klick-Zen with no hosting on your side; or
    • A URL you designate: your own support site, a knowledge base article, a survey, or any other page appropriate to the workflow.

From the customer's perspective, the whole exchange is click, then confirmation page. From your system's perspective, the ticket has been updated, tagged, routed, and any downstream automations have already fired, all in the time it takes the landing page to load.


Hosting: What Klick-Zen Serves So You Don't Have To#

Two pieces of infrastructure that would normally require your own web hosting are provided by Klick-Zen out of the box:

  • Button images. Every button you design is rendered to an image and served from Klick-Zen's servers. Email clients load it like any other remote image.
  • The landing page. The post-click confirmation page is hosted and served by Klick-Zen, with your branding applied.

For a fully white-labeled experience, both can be served from a custom subdomain on your own domain (e.g., go.yourcompany.com), so customers never see a third-party address in the email link, the image source, or the landing page URL.


Customizing or Replacing the Snippet#

The generated HTML is a starting point, not a requirement. Because the mechanism is ultimately just a link, you can adapt it:

  • Use your own image. If you host your own graphics, swap the <img> source for your image while keeping the Klick-Zen action URL in the anchor. The actions fire exactly the same.
  • Use a plain text link. Prefer a hyperlinked sentence over a button? Remove the image entirely and wrap your text in the anchor instead.
  • Place multiple buttons. Paste two or more snippets side by side to present a choice. Each button carries its own action URL and its own set of actions.
  • Use your own landing page. After the button is clicked, you can redirect the user to your own web site, or to your Zendesk Help Center instead of the Klick-Zen hosted landing page.

The only essential element is the action link with its placeholder intact; everything around it is presentation.
