# Landing Pages: what customers see after the click

An animated walkthrough, about three minutes

25 lines, 506 words (about 202 seconds of narration at 150 words per minute).

## Introduction

1. When a customer clicks a Klick-Zen button in your email, what do they see next? `intro-1`
2. A landing page. In the next few minutes, we'll make it look and sound like your brand, with no web design needed. `intro-2`

## Why landing pages matter

1. Clicking any link in an email opens a web browser, and Klick-Zen buttons are no different. That visit is what tells Klick-Zen to update the ticket. `why-1`
2. The landing page is what your customer sees next: clear confirmation that their click worked, instead of a blank tab or an error. `why-2`
3. Klick-Zen hosts it for you, and it reads clearly on phones and desktops alike. There's nothing to build or host yourself. `why-3`

## Step 1: Open the landing page settings

1. To make it yours, open Klick-Zen in Zendesk, and click Default Landing Page in the left-hand menu. `open-1`
2. There are two pages here, a Confirmation page and a Ticket Closed page, and one set of global settings that styles them both. `open-2`

## Step 2: Match your brand

1. Start with the Global Landing Page Settings. The HTML Browser Title is the text in the browser tab, usually your company name. `brand-1`
2. Next, the Template Colors. The Brand Color draws the bold outlines, so it's usually the darker of your colors. `brand-2`
3. The Accent Color sits behind the text, and the Text Color is the text itself. Choose a pair that's easy to read. `brand-3`
4. Then the Page Header Image, usually your logo. Click Select new image, and choose a JPG, GIF or PNG up to 3 megabytes. `brand-4`

## Step 3: Write your confirmation

1. Now the Confirmation page. It's shown while the ticket is still active, to let your customer know their response went through. `confirm-1`
2. Type your message on the left using simple markdown, and the preview on the right shows how it will look. `confirm-2`
3. The toolbar adds common formatting for you, such as bold text or a bulleted list. `confirm-3`
4. For security, active content like HTML, JavaScript and CSS is never rendered, so the page stays safe for every customer. `confirm-4`

## Step 4: Handle closed tickets

1. The Ticket Closed page appears when the ticket is no longer active, for example when it's already been solved and closed. `closed-1`
2. It lets your customer know their click can't change anything now, so a helpful next step here goes a long way. `closed-2`

## Step 5: Save and preview

1. The Preview this page button stays greyed out until your changes are saved. So click Update, at the bottom of the page. `save-1`
2. Now click Preview this page, and see the finished page exactly as your customers will. `save-2`

## What your customer sees

1. So when your customer clicks a button in your email, `result-1`
2. they land on a page with your logo, your colors and your words, in a browser tab with your company's name. `result-2`
3. And if they click a button on a ticket that's already closed, your Ticket Closed page tells them what to do next. `result-3`

## Next steps

1. That's landing pages: a clear, friendly answer to every click, in your own brand, with nothing to host. `outro-1`
2. Want to go further? Use your own subdomain, or send customers to a page of your own, such as a help article or a survey, for every button or just one. `outro-2`
3. Find Klick-Zen on the Zendesk Marketplace, and make every click feel like part of your brand. `outro-3`
